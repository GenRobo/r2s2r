Approach

Three kinds of validity,
checked in order.

Image-to-3D pipelines pass their errors along unchecked, generated assets lack articulation and contact, and even a plausible simulator can make a pretrained policy act differently than it does on the real robot. Before simulation can improve a deployed policy, it has to reproduce that policy’s behavior.

03

Behavioral fidelity

The frozen π0.5 policy is the discriminator: compare its actions at matched real and simulated states and correct the simulator until it reproduces real behavior. Then learn a small residual in simulation.

Objective

θ
simulator parameters: camera, appearance, embodiment
πfrozen
the pretrained robot policy, held fixed
oreal,osim
corresponding real and simulated observations
s
matched robot state and task conditioning
D
discrepancy between the policy’s action predictions

We minimize behavioral discrepancy, not maximize task success in simulation.

→

The first objective reproduces the policy’s real-world behavior, including its failures. Only after alignment do we improve the policy, through a learned residual Δφ.

Results / Policy-guided alignment

Valid geometry,
wrong behavior.

The first render passed every geometric and physical check, yet the frozen plug policy descended from its home state in 6 of 12 samples, against 12 of 12 on real frames. Each candidate change was screened with that one-inference probe and kept only if the policy’s response moved toward the real one.

Results / Residual adaptation

Improve the policy
without retraining it.

Two tasks, two independently fine-tuned π0.5 checkpoints, two ways of collecting data: plug insertion from Jello-style teleoperation, camera pickup from UMI-style handheld demonstrations. The base policy stays frozen; a small residual learns bounded corrections from simulated oracle demonstrations.
